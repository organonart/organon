### The CouchStation — a design capture for the reclined, voice-primary workstation

`doc/couchstation.md` records the reasoning behind the form factor that the last two
builds of the author's own workstation have converged on: reclined and fully supported,
two hands at two *independent* heights, voice carrying content, a near screen and a far
projection, and no desk anywhere in the room. It carries the fifteen-pattern language
(written in Christopher Alexander's form, deliberately — he is the origin of "design
patterns" in software, so a document bridging a workstation and an operating system using
his structure closes a loop open since 1977), the anthropometric basis, the input model
including the analog-throttle idea, and the tier and certification structure that a
published specification would rest on.

🚨 **Its honesty ledger has an empty Measured column, and that is the most important fact
about it.** The document is Observed, Derived and Argued throughout — fine for a design
brief and disqualifying for a standard. §10 lists the seven numbers per build that have to
be taken with a tape measure before any of this becomes a spec, and notes that they are
perishable: the first build is already gone and survives only as photographs containing a
panel of known width.

📌 The claim the whole thing rests on is that **a desk is not furniture, it is ergonomic
infrastructure for a hand problem** — every dimension of every desk descends from the
requirement that both hands sit coplanar and coequal for hours, which is a *typing*
requirement. Remove it and the furniture is not replaced, it is obsoleted. The two
load-bearing patterns are Independent Hand Planes, which removes the desk, and The Trigger
Under The Thumb, which is what makes the first one survivable past a day.

The document is written to be lifted: §5 becomes `PATTERNS.md` in `organonart/couchstation`
unchanged, and §11 records the repo/site/governance shape that spin-off would take, plus
what was deliberately deferred and why.
